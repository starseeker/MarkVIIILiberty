# Modern Reference Sources

Modern photographs and video of surviving Mark VIII tanks are not substitutes for the period handbooks and parts catalogs. They are nevertheless valuable observational sources: they show how a surviving vehicle is actually assembled, expose surfaces hidden in most photographs, and help distinguish a plausible reconstruction from an arrangement that only works on paper.

Modern material should remain separate from the period primary-source hierarchy. It should be cited as an observation of a particular surviving vehicle, at a particular date and in a particular restoration state. It must not silently override the Preliminary Handbook or SNL G-13 when the question concerns the intended 1918 design.

## Video: Two 100 year old tanks moved across Fort Benning

**Source:** [Internet Archive mirror of the video](https://archive.org/details/youtube-Gu5tctNJzIY)  
**Original title:** *Two 100 year old tanks (Mk. VIII and Mark V*) lifted and moved across Ft. Benning*  
**Original creator/channel:** Sofilein  
**Date:** 2022  
**Subject:** An American Mark VIII and a Mark V* being lifted and moved across the U.S. Army Armor & Cavalry Collection at Fort Benning, now Fort Moore.

The video is a modern museum and collection record, not period test footage. The Archive item retains a 3840×2160 WebM original as well as a much smaller 854×480 MP4 derivative. The 4K original should be preserved and used for frame extraction; the MP4 is convenient for viewing but is a poor choice for detailed visual analysis.

The Mark VIII portion is useful because the tank is shown from multiple exterior angles, lifted clear of the ground, moved on a trailer, placed in a building, and briefly viewed from inside. The latter portion of the video concerns the Mark V* and should be cataloged separately if individual frames are extracted.

## Why it matters

The video is most useful as a **modern observational reference for an American-built, postwar-service Mark VIII**. The U.S. Army identifies the surviving Fort Benning example as a Rock Island Arsenal-built tank completed in 1920 and used by the Army during the interwar period. That makes it a particularly relevant physical comparison for the American production configuration, while also making its variant and service history important qualifications. See the U.S. Army's [Mark VIII historical article](https://www.lineofdeparture.army.mil/Journals/Army-History/Archive/Summer-2024-Issue/MarkVIII/).

The strongest uses are:

- **Exterior construction:** plate boundaries, rivet rows, overlaps, hinges, brackets, doors, vision ports, fittings, and the relationship between the upper hull and track frames;
- **Sponsons and side openings:** the shape of the sponson armor, gun-opening area, access doors, machine-gun positions, and surrounding interior clearances;
- **Running gear:** track shoes, track framing, idler and drive-end geometry, mud chutes, lower hull edges, and details visible from low angles;
- **Hidden surfaces:** portions of the lower hull and underside become visible while the vehicle is suspended by the crane;
- **Interior context:** bulkheads, door openings, riveted framing, and the spatial relationship between the fighting compartment and rear machinery spaces;
- **Restoration and variant study:** missing, replaced, modified, or preserved components on a known American survivor; and
- **Visual validation:** checking whether CAD assemblies produce a credible silhouette, clearance arrangement, and distribution of visible fittings.

These observations are difficult to obtain from the Preliminary Handbook alone. The handbook can show the intended arrangement, while the video shows how a surviving vehicle's plates, fasteners, openings, and access spaces coexist in three dimensions.

## What the video cannot establish by itself

The footage should not be used as an unqualified source of exact dimensions or original design intent.

- The handheld camera has unknown lens calibration, perspective distortion, and changing distance from the tank.
- The vehicle is being lifted and transported, not driven under its own power. The footage therefore provides little evidence about suspension travel, steering, track behavior, drivetrain operation, or engine performance.
- The surviving tank represents an American vehicle with an interwar service history, not necessarily the 1918 prototype or the British configuration.
- Current paint, repairs, replacement fasteners, missing guns, altered fittings, and museum restoration work must not automatically be treated as original.
- A dimension inferred from a video frame is weak unless calibrated against a known dimension and confirmed by period documentation or another independent observation.
- The video does not provide a complete survey of every interior compartment or hidden surface.

The correct interpretation is therefore: **direct observation of one surviving vehicle in a modern condition**, not direct evidence of every Mark VIII ever built.

## Relationship to the period sources

The video should be used alongside the repository's period sources in the following way:

| Question | Preferred source | Role of the video |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overall design and intended layout | Preliminary Handbook | Validate visible proportions and identify possible discrepancies |
| Parts names, quantities, and assemblies | SNL G-13 | Identify what is visible and record service-variant differences |
| Tank-specific engine installation | Preliminary Handbook and SNL G-13 | Confirm access, clearances, mounts, and visible installation context |
| Base Liberty engine geometry | Liberty engine handbook | Little direct authority; use only for comparison with visible engine features |
| 6-pounder and tank mounting | Hotchkiss handbook plus tank sources | Confirm actual surviving placement and surrounding armor, not gun dimensions alone |
| Exact dimensions | Printed period dimensions and calibrated plates | Provide visual checks or possible scale references, never sole authority |

The Preliminary Handbook remains primary for overall structure and geometry. SNL G-13 remains the principal parts-hierarchy and nomenclature supplement. The modern video is an observational layer that can reveal restoration state, production/service variation, and three-dimensional relationships that the documents leave ambiguous.

## Recommended extraction and analysis workflow

1. Preserve the original 4K WebM and record the Archive identifier, original YouTube URL, title, date, and checksum.
2. Extract frames at timestamped intervals, retaining the original frame time in the filename or sidecar metadata.
3. Separate Mark VIII frames from Mark V* frames before adding them to the reference index.
4. Select stable views of the port side, starboard side, front, rear, track runs, sponsons, underside, and interior.
5. Correct lens or perspective distortion only in a derived working copy; never replace the original frame.
6. Use known handbook dimensions to test rough scale, but label any resulting measurement as video-scaled or visually estimated.
7. Compare observations against the Preliminary Handbook and SNL G-13, recording whether each feature is confirmed, variant-specific, restored, missing, or unresolved.
8. If photogrammetry is attempted, use it for an exterior surface or proportion check rather than treating the resulting mesh as authoritative hidden geometry.

## Suggested provenance record

Each extracted frame or observation should retain at least:

```text
SourceType: modern-observational-video
SourceTitle: Two 100 year old tanks (Mk. VIII and Mark V*) lifted and moved across Ft. Benning
ArchiveIdentifier: youtube-Gu5tctNJzIY
OriginalDate: 2022
Vehicle: American Mark VIII, unless otherwise noted
Timestamp: HH:MM:SS
FrameFile: <derived frame filename>
View: port / starboard / front / rear / underside / interior / unknown
EvidenceStatus: observed-variant / visual-check / video-scaled / unresolved
Notes: restoration state, missing components, or relevant uncertainty
```

The source should be treated as especially useful for **visual approximation**, **observed variant**, and **assembly-context** evidence. It should not be used to convert an unverified appearance into a confirmed 1918 dimension.

Period motion-picture sources are cataloged separately in [primary-sources.md](primary-sources.md). In particular, the 1918 Imperial War Museums film and the U.S. Ordnance test footage provide contemporary evidence of prototype trials and movement, whereas this 2022 video documents a surviving American vehicle in a modern museum and transport context.
