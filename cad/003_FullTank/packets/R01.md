# R01 — pressed track shoe and complete connecting-link unit

Status: **active**, intermediate component reconstruction. Rock Island first-100
configuration; handbook/SNL production applicability remains a documented transfer.
Owning subsystem: RunningGear, under PortRunningGear and StarboardRunningGear.

The deliverable is the complete repeated shoe/link unit, followed by qualification
at the bends required by R03. The original trial used three units per side;
[R03](R03.md) now instantiates 78 units per side with closed pin joints.
Wheel engagement and support-contact qualification remain open.

## Geometry and interfaces

Local X follows the straight ground run, Y spans shoe width, and Z points inward
from the shoe ground face. Unit datums are separated by printed pitch. Each unit
owns the pin and bushing at its positive-X joint; the adjacent unit contributes
its opposite end eyes, avoiding duplicate pin ownership.

The shoe starts with a native longitudinal-section sketch and pad, then receives
rounded plan corners, a central quartic NURBS pressing and eight rivet bores.
The pressing uses clamped degree-four surfaces with a flat boundary and tangent.
Only the center pole has nonzero displacement; its height is calculated to
produce the declared surface peak. Top and bottom surfaces have constant vertical
separation, an approximation to formed sheet of constant normal thickness.
The patch is a native solid feature, not a mesh or a fitted photograph.

Handed bars use a web and pin eyes, stepped half-width end laps, outward feet and
pin/rivet bores. Production forging radii, crank contours and local thickness
changes are not yet recovered. Bushings and pins use analytic cylindrical
surfaces; pin cross-holes receive bent cotters. Rivets use a cylindrical shank,
pair of equal spherical buttons. The provisional underside form requires
further evidence.

The initial flush-tail trial did not conserve the untrimmed stock-shank volume.
It was replaced by the two-button approximation. A 34 mm button diameter and
11.5 mm height imply 32.027208 mm grip from the printed stock size; subtracting
the 8 mm shoe gives a provisional 24.027208 mm forged-foot thickness. These
correlated quantities are parameter expressions. The native builder checks stock
volume conservation. This is an engineering reconstruction, not evidence that the
historical underside had this exact head form. Issue `track_fastener_form` retains
that limitation. The unit datum lifts the shoe by the 11.5 mm underside-head
height, placing the lowest head points on the nominal ground plane.

All approximate dimensions and their bounds are in parameters.json. Principal
assumptions are 8 mm shoe thickness, 14 mm lip rise, 18 mm overlap, 60 mm pin
height, 380 mm channel spacing and 104 mm bar spacing within each channel.
These are reconstruction parameters, not manufacturing tolerances.

The nominal clearances are 0.4 mm between stepped end laps, 0.2 mm between each
bushing end and its inner bar face, 0.3 mm diametral bushing/pin clearance and
0.2 mm diametral pin-eye/rivet-hole clearance. Numerical intersection tolerance
is separately 0.001 mm³. Initial checks covered the nominal straight installation;
the entire parameter-bound box is not asserted to be feasible.

## Verification and open work

The data checks compare template leaf counts against the frozen SNL BOM and
reject a deliberately missing link bar. Native build, material intersection,
NURBS retention, STEP round trip, relative relocation and repeated pitch checks
are recorded in generated reports. A generated report is authoritative only for
its matching input fingerprint; later changes invalidate it.

R01 remains active until the part shapes have been visually reviewed and their
joint/lip geometry qualified over the bend angles required by R03. The source
does not establish precise forged contours, pin fits, underside rivet heads or
the pressed dome profile. The current seven definitions retain `partial` coverage.

Nominal straight-segment checks pass for 114 component instances and 316 candidate
contact pairs. The shoes retain two NURBS faces each. Two-unit sampled bend trials
are clear at −5°, 0°, 10°, 20°, 30° and 35°. At −10° reverse bend the overlapping
shoes intersect by approximately 282 mm³. These discrete samples do not establish
a continuous permitted range; the required route angles remain an R03 dependency.

Full-assembly validation also passed native/STEP reopening, relative relocation,
independent rebuild and unchanged cache reuse. A +0.5 mm pitch perturbation
changed shoe/bar lengths, moved joint hardware and advanced successive units by
the correct accumulated distance; unrelated geometry stayed fixed. The perturbed
fit itself is not claimed qualified. See `build/reports/validation.json` and
`build/reports/track_components.json` for the matching final input version.

HB130's stated road-wheel OD and 35 teeth, HB133's 3.735 in pitch, HB119's 37-tooth
description, and HB137's track pitch do not yet establish a consistent contact
circle. Issue `drive_pitch_conflict` belongs to R02/R03; it must be resolved or
explicitly approximated without silently altering the printed shoe pitch.
